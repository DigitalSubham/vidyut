# Bihar Expansion Plan (Post-Patna)

**Prepared:** July 2026 · Companion to `BIHAR_MARKET_FEASIBILITY.md`
**Precondition:** Do not begin multi-city expansion until Patna clears the Go/No-Go scorecard (paid renewals, adoption >70%, support <2 hrs/school/week). Expansion multiplies both revenue *and* support cost — sequence it by **support feasibility**, not just market size.

---

## 1. Expansion Principle: Support Economics Drive Sequence

A low-ticket ERP cannot afford a field team in every city. So expansion follows two motions:

- **Motion A — Field extension (near Patna):** cities within a ~2-hour drive can be served by the Patna team + a local rep. Full-touch selling and onsite go-live remain possible.
- **Motion B — Hub-and-spoke / remote + partner (far cities):** distant cities are entered via **remote onboarding + a local reseller/consultant partner** who provides the physical trust and first-line support, while we run product + escalation. Lower touch, lower margin per school, but scalable without a full branch.

Match each city to a motion (last column of the scorecard). Getting this wrong (full field team in a far city at ₹20k ARPU) destroys unit economics.

## 3. Recommended Expansion Sequence

**Wave 1 (Year 2, Motion A — field extension from Patna):** **Hajipur, Bihar Sharif, Ara** (and Sasaram if convenient on the GT-road corridor). Adjacent, day-trip-able, same team, onsite go-live still feasible. Proves the "extend without a branch" model.

**Wave 2 (Year 2–3, Motion B — hub + local partner):** **Gaya** and **Muzaffarpur** — the two biggest non-Patna markets. Recruit a local reseller/consultant per city for trust + first-line support; we run product + escalation + remote onboarding. These are the volume prizes.

**Wave 3 (Year 3+):** **Bhagalpur, Darbhanga, Begusarai**, then Chhapra/Samastipur. Partner-led.

**Wave 4 (opportunistic/later):** Purnia, Katihar, Motihari, Bettiah, Munger, Siwan — remote + partner only, or inbound-driven, once the product is self-serve enough to onboard with minimal touch.

**Guardrail:** enter a new city only when the previous wave's schools are stable (churn in check, support burden per school within target). Never let expansion outrun support capacity.

## 4. Operational Model by Motion

| Dimension | Motion A (near Patna) | Motion B (far cities) |
|---|---|---|
| Selling | Patna team + local rep, in-person | Local partner + remote demo/close |
| Onboarding | Onsite go-live | Remote + partner assist; occasional visit |
| Support | WhatsApp/phone from Patna hub | Partner first-line + our escalation |
| Pricing | Standard packages | Standard; partner margin baked in |
| Margin impact | Similar to Patna | Lower per school (partner cut) but no branch cost |
| Trust builder | Our local rep | Local partner's face + our SLA |

**Central hub (Patna) provides for all cities:** product, data migration tooling, support escalation, billing, case studies, training content (Hindi video library), and a partner-enablement kit.

---

## 5. Partner Strategy for Remote Cities

- **Who:** local IT/computer dealers, education consultants, CAs serving schools, existing website/hardware vendors with school relationships (see `PATNA_GO_TO_MARKET_PLAN.md` §7).
- **Deal:** referral fee or reseller margin (e.g., 15–25% of Yr-1, lower on renewal), we retain product/support/billing; partner does local trust + first-line help.
- **Enablement:** demo kit, Hindi training videos, a partner portal, co-branded collateral, lead-registration to avoid conflicts.
- **Risk controls:** clear lead ownership, support-responsibility split in writing, quality bar (drop non-performing partners), no exclusive territories until proven.

**Reading it:**
- **Base case reaches operating break-even in Year 2–3 at ~100–140 schools**, consistent with the unit-economics file.
- **Conservative** is a slow-burn that still turns profitable by Year 3 — the downside is survivable, not catastrophic, *if* burn is controlled and pricing holds.
- **Aggressive** requires faster hiring + partner leverage and is sensitive to churn/support quality.
- **The whole forecast collapses under two failure modes:** (1) reverting to ₹299-style pricing (gross profit turns negative — see `PRICING_AND_UNIT_ECONOMICS.md`), or (2) support cost per school not falling with scale (adoption/onboarding failure). Both are operational, not market, risks.

**No false precision:** these are scenario sketches on `[Estimated]` inputs. The Year-1 pilot must replace ARPU, churn, CAC, and support-hours assumptions with measured values before Year-2 hiring commitments.

## 7. What to Validate Before Each Wave `[Field]`

- Real ICP-fit school counts per target city (mini-census).
- Local competitive density (which vendors already there).
- Availability and quality of a local partner.
- Whether remote onboarding actually achieves >70% adoption without onsite presence (test in Wave 1 before committing to far cities).
- Per-city payback and support-hours — expand only where they match Patna benchmarks.
