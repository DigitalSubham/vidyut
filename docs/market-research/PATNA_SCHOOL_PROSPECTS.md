# Patna School Prospects — Methodology, Map & Priority Framework

**Prepared:** July 2026 · Companion to `BIHAR_MARKET_FEASIBILITY.md`

> **Ethics & data rules (read first):** This file provides a **prospecting method and a public, business-level framework** — not a scraped database of private contact details. School *names, areas, and board affiliations* are public business facts. **Phone numbers, emails, staff names, and student counts must be gathered from schools' own public listings (their website/office board) or in person, and are marked `[Field — collect ethically]`.** Do **not** fabricate contact data. Do **not** buy or scrape personal data. Outreach must be relevant, consented, and legal (see `PATNA_GO_TO_MARKET_PLAN.md`).

## 1. Prospecting Methodology

**Step 1 — Build the frame (desk).** Compile publicly listed private schools in Patna district from: CBSE affiliation directory (saras.cbse.gov.in), UDISE+ school directory, school-listing sites (edustoke, schools.org.in, careers360) as *leads only*, and Google Maps by area. Capture only: name, area, board, website (if public).

**Step 2 — Enrich (desk + light Field).** From each school's **own public website/office**, note: approximate size, whether they show a parent-login/app link, online-payment link, online-admission form, and any ERP "powered by" footer. These reveal digital maturity without any private data.

**Step 3 — Score & prioritise.** Apply the priority rubric (§4) to rank into A/B/C tiers.

**Step 4 — Physical census of target clusters (Field).** Walk 2–3 high-density areas; log signboards, size cues (building/gate/parking), and whether they advertise "smart classes / app." This is where the *real* mid-market ICP list (which is not fully online) gets built.

**Step 5 — Warm-intro mapping.** For each A-tier prospect, find a path in (known principal, vendor, CA, parent). Cold-walk only where no warm path exists.

**Why physical census matters:** the famous CBSE schools are online and easy to list — but they're the *wrong* first targets (premium, incumbent-locked). The **mid-market ICP (200–800 students, owner-run, English-medium)** is under-represented online and must be found on the ground. Directory lists over-index on premium; the ICP is the long tail you see on the street.

## 3. Digital-Maturity Classification

Score each prospect 0–5; higher = more ready to adopt (but also more likely already on something). The **sweet spot is level 1–3**: enough digital comfort to adopt, not so much they're locked into a competitor.

| Level | Signal | ERP-readiness | Sales approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — Fully manual | Registers, paper receipts, no website | High pain, low readiness | Sell simplicity + onsite hand-holding; slower |
| 1 — Excel/Sheets | Fees in Excel, WhatsApp for parents | **Ideal** | Lead with fee automation ROI |
| 2 — WhatsApp-heavy + basic site | Broadcast groups, static website | **Ideal** | Lead with structured comms + fees |
| 3 — Basic desktop/local app | Old billing software, partial digitisation | **Good** (upgrade sell) | Migrate + modernise + mobile app |
| 4 — Existing local ERP | Chanakya/Orataro/etc., some app | Harder (switching) | Compete on reliability/support/price-value |
| 5 — Modern national ERP | Entab/MyClassBoard/Teachmint live | **Avoid early** | Deprioritise; revisit at renewal only |

**Rule of thumb:** target levels **1–3** first. Level 0 is high-effort; levels 4–5 are switching battles best left until you have references.

## 4. Priority Scoring Rubric

Score each prospect (max 100). A-tier ≥70, B-tier 45–69, C-tier <45.

| Factor | Weight | 5-point anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Size fit (200–800 students) | 25 | 5 = 250–600; 1 = <100 or >1,500 |
| Digital maturity (level 1–3) | 20 | 5 = Excel/WhatsApp; 1 = on national ERP |
| Owner-operated / single decision-maker | 15 | 5 = owner signs & answers phone |
| Fee-collection pain signal | 15 | 5 = visible defaulter/receipt pain |
| Reachability (warm intro / area) | 10 | 5 = warm intro in A-area |
| English-medium / CBSE-aspiring | 10 | 5 = English-medium CBSE-track |
| Ability to pay (₹15k+/yr) | 5 | 5 = healthy fees/enrolment |

Compute during census; maintain a living CRM sheet with these columns:
`Name | Area | District | Board | Type | Est. size [Field] | Website | Public office contact [Field] | # Branches | Uses ERP? [Field] | ERP/app name [Field] | Online fee pay? | Parent app? | Online admission? | Digital level (0–5) | Decision-maker role | Priority score | A/B/C | Reason | Warm-intro path`

## 6. Patna-Periphery Prospect Frame (Phase 4)

Belt reachable within ~30–45 min of central Patna, sellable once the core is proven:

**Danapur, Khagaul, Phulwari Sharif, Saguna More, Bihta (emerging), Fatuha, Naubatpur.**

- Expect more **low-fee + Hindi-medium** schools → smaller sizes, lower WTP → position **Starter/Standard**, heavier onsite reliance.
- Cluster visits (do 3–4 schools per trip) to keep travel cost per prospect down.
- Use core-Patna references as proof; recruit a local rep once >15 schools here.

## 7. Wider-Bihar Expansion Prospect Framework (Phase 5)

Do **not** build city prospect lists until Patna is proven. When ready, replicate the method per city, prioritising by the **Bihar City Expansion Scorecard** (see `BIHAR_EXPANSION_PLAN.md`): Gaya, Muzaffarpur, Bhagalpur, Bihar Sharif, Darbhanga first. For remote cities, favour **remote onboarding + local reseller/partner** over own field team (support-economics reason).
